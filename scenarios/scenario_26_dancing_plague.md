### RISING ACTION (Scenes 9-15)

**Scene 9**
- Narration: "The fifteen eighteen outbreak was not the first dancing plague. Historical records document at least seven similar events between the fourteenth and seventeenth centuries — primarily in the Rhine region of Europe. In thirteen seventy-four, dozens of people danced on a bridge over the Mosel River in Aachen. In fourteen sixty-three, an outbreak struck Trier. The phenomenon had a geographic concentration and a historical pattern."
- Image Prompt: "Map of the Rhine region showing locations of dancing plagues across centuries — Aachen 1374, Strasbourg 1518, Trier 1463 — the geographic clustering visible, medieval cartography style, cinematic photorealistic, 4K"
- Subtitle: "7+ OUTBREAKS — 14TH TO 17TH CENTURY — CONCENTRATED IN THE RHINE REGION"
- Ken Burns: Slow zoom in on the clustered locations

**Scene 10**
- Narration: "Historian John Waller — the leading modern authority on the event — has argued convincingly that the dancing plague was a case of mass psychogenic illness: a stress-induced trance state in which cultural beliefs provided the template for symptoms. The people of Strasbourg believed that Saint Vitus could curse people with dancing. They feared it. And in times of extreme stress — that fear became reality."

**Scene 11**
- Narration: "Mass psychogenic illness — formerly called mass hysteria — is a documented medical phenomenon in which psychological distress manifests as physical symptoms that spread through a group. Modern examples include fainting epidemics in schools, unexplained illness clusters in factories, and episodes of uncontrollable shaking or movement in stressed communities. The mechanism is real — the brain can produce genuine physical symptoms in response to psychological triggers."

**Scene 12**
- Narration: "In this framework, Frau Troffea's dancing was an individual psychogenic response to extreme stress — her body expressing psychological anguish through involuntary movement. The spread to others occurred through psychological contagion — witnesses who shared the same stresses and cultural beliefs entered the same trance state when they observed the behavior. Each new dancer validated and reinforced the phenomenon for others."
- Image Prompt: "Ripple effect visualization showing one dancer at the center, concentric circles spreading outward with more dancers appearing in each ring, the contagion of psychogenic illness spreading through observation, cinematic photorealistic, 4K"

This belief was reinforced by centuries of church teaching and previous outbreaks. When Frau Troffea began dancing — the population had a ready-made explanation: the saint's curse. And by believing the curse was real, they made themselves vulnerable to it. The fear of dancing became the cause of dancing."

And ergot typically affects entire communities simultaneously — not individuals who join one by one over weeks. The symptoms don't match the pattern."

Belief caused it. Belief cured it."
