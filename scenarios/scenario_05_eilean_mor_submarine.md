Neither did the thirteen-man rescue plane sent to find them. In a single afternoon, six aircraft and twenty-seven men vanished over the Atlantic Ocean."

- Narration: "Flight Nineteen was a standard overwater navigation training exercise assigned to fourteen naval aviators based at the Fort Lauderdale Naval Air Station. ### RISING ACTION (Scenes 9-15)

**Scene 9**
- Narration: "Taylor's belief that he was over the Keys was catastrophic. If he was actually northeast of the Bahamas — where he was supposed to be — then flying north or northeast to find land would make sense. But if he believed he was over the Keys, his instinct would be to fly northeast to reach the Florida mainland. In reality, northeast would take him further out over the open Atlantic."

**Scene 10**
- Narration: "Radio transmissions were intermittent but increasingly alarming. At four-twenty-five, Taylor was heard telling his students: 'If we would just fly west, we would get home.' One of his student pilots apparently suggested exactly that. But Taylor overruled him. He continued flying northeast — deeper into the Atlantic. His certainty about his position overrode the correct instincts of his students."

### CLIMAX (Scenes 16-22)

**Scene 16**
- Narration: "The SS Gaines Mills, a merchant ship in the area, reported seeing a massive explosion in the sky at approximately seven-fifty that evening — the time and location consistent with the Mariner's last known position. A large patch of oil and debris was later found on the surface. The Mariner had exploded in midair. But the cause was never conclusively determined."

**Scene 17**
- Narration: "PBM Mariners were known to have fuel vapor issues. The aircraft had earned the nickname 'flying gas tanks' among Navy crews. An electrical spark, a fuel leak, a mechanical failure — any could have caused an in-flight explosion. The Mariner's loss, while tragic, likely had a conventional explanation. But Flight Nineteen's disappearance did not."

- Narration: "The search for Flight Nineteen became one of the largest air and sea searches in history. Five aircraft carrying fourteen men had simply vanished from the face of the earth."

- Narration: "The Navy's official investigation concluded that Taylor had become disoriented and led his flight in the wrong direction until they ran out of fuel over the deep Atlantic. Ditching a TBM Avenger at night in rough seas was virtually unsurvivable — the aircraft sank within seconds. The Atlantic at that point was over five thousand feet deep. The planes would never be found."

- Narration: "But the Navy's conclusion raised difficult questions. Why did both of Taylor's compasses fail simultaneously? Why couldn't ground stations establish a reliable fix on the flight's radio transmissions? Why did the rescue plane explode? Details were exaggerated. Supernatural explanations proliferated. The real tragedy became obscured by mythology."

**Scene 22**
- Narration: "Stripped of mythology, the most likely explanation is tragically mundane: a compass failure led an experienced but area-unfamiliar pilot to become disoriented. His incorrect assumption about his position caused him to fly in the wrong direction. His students trusted his experience. They flew until the fuel ran out. They ditched in deep water at night. The ocean swallowed them. It explains almost everything. Almost."
