## Genre: Unexplained Event / Anomaly

### HOOK (Scenes 1-3)

**Scene 1**
- Narration: "On August fifteenth, nineteen seventy-seven, a radio telescope in Ohio received a signal from deep space that should not have existed. It lasted exactly seventy-two seconds. It was thirty times stronger than the background noise of the universe. It came from the direction of the constellation Sagittarius — and it has never been detected again. The astronomer who found it circled the data printout in red ink and wrote a single word in the margin: 'Wow!'"

- Subtitle: "AUGUST 15, 1977 — 72 SECONDS — ONE WORD: 'WOW!'"
- Ken Burns: Slow zoom in on the circled sequence

**Scene 2**
- Narration: "For forty-nine years, scientists have searched for this signal again. They have pointed radio telescopes at the same coordinates hundreds of times. They have listened for thousands of hours. They have used instruments far more sensitive than the one that detected it. The signal has never repeated. It appeared once — and only once — like a voice calling across the cosmos that spoke a single sentence and then fell silent forever."
- Image Prompt: "Large radio telescope dish pointed at a star-filled night sky, the constellation Sagittarius visible in the direction the dish is aimed, observation building with dim lights, deep night atmosphere, cinematic photorealistic, 4K"
- Subtitle: "SEARCHED FOR 49 YEARS — NEVER DETECTED AGAIN"
- Ken Burns: Slow zoom toward the stars in Sagittarius

**Scene 3**
- Narration: "The Wow! Signal remains the strongest candidate for an extraterrestrial radio transmission ever detected. It matched every criterion that scientists had established for what an alien signal should look like. Narrowband. Powerful. At the frequency of hydrogen — the most logical frequency for interstellar communication. It was exactly what we were looking for. And we have no explanation for what it was."
- Image Prompt: "Radio frequency spectrum visualization showing a massive spike at the 1420 MHz hydrogen line, the spike towering above the flat background noise, scientific data visualization with frequency labels, dramatic dark background, cinematic photorealistic, 4K"
- Subtitle: "THE STRONGEST CANDIDATE FOR AN ALIEN TRANSMISSION — EVER"
- Ken Burns: Slow zoom in on the frequency spike

### SETUP (Scenes 4-8)

**Scene 4**
- Narration: "The signal was detected by the Big Ear radio telescope at Ohio State University — a massive fixed antenna the size of three football fields. Big Ear was part of the SETI program — the Search for Extraterrestrial Intelligence — scanning the sky for artificial radio signals that could indicate technologically advanced civilizations. It had been searching since nineteen seventy-three without finding anything unusual."
- Image Prompt: "The Big Ear radio telescope at Ohio State University — a large flat ground-based radio antenna with reflector panels stretching across a field, university buildings in the background, 1970s era, clear sky, scientific purpose evident, cinematic photorealistic, 4K"
- Subtitle: "BIG EAR — OHIO STATE — SEARCHING SINCE 1973"
- Ken Burns: Slow pan across the massive antenna

**Scene 5**
- Narration: "The telescope operated by slowly scanning the sky as the Earth rotated. Any celestial radio source would appear in the telescope's beam for exactly seventy-two seconds as the Earth turned past it. This was critical — a genuine extraterrestrial signal would rise in intensity for thirty-six seconds as it entered the beam, peak, and then fade for thirty-six seconds as it exited. The Wow! Signal followed this pattern perfectly."
- Image Prompt: "Diagram showing how the Big Ear telescope scans the sky through Earth's rotation, a signal appearing and fading over exactly 72 seconds, the bell curve shape of detection matching expectations, scientific illustration, cinematic photorealistic, 4K"
- Subtitle: "72 SECONDS — THE SIGNAL MATCHED THE TELESCOPE'S BEAM PATTERN PERFECTLY"
- Ken Burns: Slow zoom in on the bell curve pattern

**Scene 6**
- Narration: "The astronomer who discovered the signal was Jerry Ehman — a volunteer professor who analyzed the telescope's data on computer printouts. He was reviewing data from days earlier when he spotted the anomaly on August eighteenth — three days after the signal was received. His red ink notation would become the most famous annotation in the history of astronomy."
- Image Prompt: "1970s era astronomer's office with stacks of continuous-feed computer paper, a man in glasses leaning over a desk examining printouts with a red pen, desk lamp illuminating the work, books and papers everywhere, cinematic photorealistic, 4K"
- Subtitle: "JERRY EHMAN — FOUND IT 3 DAYS LATER ON A PRINTOUT"
- Ken Burns: Slow zoom in on the man examining the paper

**Scene 7**
- Narration: "The data sequence that Ehman circled reads: six-E-Q-U-J-five. Each character represents the signal intensity during a twelve-second interval. The scale runs from one to nine, then continues with letters — A through Z — for higher intensities. The letter U represents an intensity of thirty — meaning the signal was thirty times louder than the background. No natural source in that direction could explain such intensity."
- Image Prompt: "Close-up of the alphanumeric scale used by Big Ear, showing 1-9 then A-Z, with U highlighted and labeled as '30x background noise', the sequence 6EQUJ5 displayed prominently, technical but clear explanation, cinematic photorealistic, 4K"
- Subtitle: "'U' = 30 TIMES BACKGROUND NOISE — UNPRECEDENTED INTENSITY"
- Ken Burns: Slow zoom in on the U character

**Scene 8**
- Narration: "The signal was received at a frequency of fourteen-twenty megahertz — the hydrogen line. This frequency is significant because hydrogen is the most abundant element in the universe. In nineteen fifty-nine, physicists Philip Morrison and Giuseppe Cocconi proposed that any intelligent civilization attempting interstellar communication would logically broadcast at this frequency — as a universal reference point. The Wow! Signal was at precisely this frequency."
- Image Prompt: "Hydrogen atom diagram with electron orbital, the 1420 MHz frequency labeled, connected to a radio spectrum showing the signal at that exact frequency, the logic of choosing this frequency for communication illustrated, cinematic photorealistic, 4K"
- Subtitle: "1420 MHz — THE HYDROGEN LINE — THE 'UNIVERSAL FREQUENCY'"
- Ken Burns: Slow zoom connecting the hydrogen atom to the frequency

### RISING ACTION (Scenes 9-15)

**Scene 9**
- Narration: "In the days following the discovery, Ehman and his colleagues pointed every available instrument at the source coordinates. They listened for weeks. Nothing. The signal did not repeat. They checked for equipment malfunction, terrestrial interference, satellite transmissions, aircraft radar — nothing matched. The signal appeared to be genuine, extraterrestrial in origin, and unique."
- Image Prompt: "Multiple radio telescope dishes pointed at the same region of the sky, nighttime observation, different facilities working together, all aimed at the same coordinates in Sagittarius, searching and finding nothing, cinematic photorealistic, 4K"
- Subtitle: "WEEKS OF LISTENING — NOTHING — THE SIGNAL NEVER REPEATED"
- Ken Burns: Slow pan across the listening dishes

**Scene 10**
- Narration: "The signal came from the direction of the star group Chi Sagittarii — approximately one hundred and twenty light-years from Earth. However, there are no known stars at the exact coordinates that could produce such a signal naturally. The source appears to be empty space — or an object too faint to detect optically."
- Image Prompt: "Star map of the Sagittarius constellation with the signal's origin point marked, the area appearing relatively empty of bright stars, a red crosshair marking the exact coordinates, deep space darkness surrounding the point, cinematic photorealistic, 4K"
- Subtitle: "DIRECTION: CHI SAGITTARII — 120 LIGHT-YEARS — NO KNOWN SOURCE"
- Ken Burns: Slow zoom toward the marked coordinates

**Scene 11**
- Narration: "Several natural explanations have been proposed over the decades. Interstellar scintillation — the twinkling of a weak source amplified by intervening gas clouds. A hydrogen cloud stimulated by a background radiation source. A gravitational lens briefly focusing a distant signal. Each theory has problems. None fully accounts for the signal's characteristics."
- Image Prompt: "Scientific diagram showing three proposed natural explanations — scintillation, hydrogen cloud emission, gravitational lensing — each with an X or question mark beside it indicating insufficiency, academic presentation style, cinematic photorealistic, 4K"
- Subtitle: "NATURAL EXPLANATIONS PROPOSED — NONE FULLY ACCOUNT FOR THE SIGNAL"
- Ken Burns: Slow pan across the three diagrams

**Scene 12**
- Narration: "In twenty seventeen, astronomer Antonio Paris proposed that the signal was caused by hydrogen clouds surrounding two comets — two-sixty-six-P-Christensen and P-two-thousand-eight-Y-two — that were in the beam's path in nineteen seventy-seven but were not catalogued at the time. He published a study claiming his observations of one comet showed a signal at fourteen-twenty megahertz."
- Image Prompt: "Comet with a visible hydrogen coma and tail against a starfield, radio frequency waves emanating from the hydrogen cloud surrounding the comet, scientific visualization of the proposed mechanism, cinematic photorealistic, 4K"
- Subtitle: "2017 — COMET HYPOTHESIS — HYDROGEN CLOUDS AROUND COMETS"
- Ken Burns: Slow zoom toward the comet's hydrogen cloud

**Scene 13**
- Narration: "The comet hypothesis was widely criticized by the scientific community. Multiple astronomers pointed out that comets do not produce signals anywhere near the intensity of the Wow! Signal. The hydrogen emission from a comet would be thousands of times too weak. The positional data was also questioned. By twenty twenty, the comet theory was largely dismissed as inadequate."
- Image Prompt: "Scientific paper with peer review comments marked in red, 'REJECTED' stamp, graphs showing signal intensity comparisons — the Wow! Signal vastly stronger than any cometary emission, academic debate captured in documents, cinematic photorealistic, 4K"
- Subtitle: "COMET THEORY CRITICIZED AND LARGELY DISMISSED — TOO WEAK"
- Ken Burns: Slow zoom in on the intensity comparison graph

**Scene 14**
- Narration: "Jerry Ehman himself remained cautious throughout his life about the signal's interpretation. He stated: 'I am not drawing conclusions about its origin. We should have seen it again when we looked for it — and we didn't. I choose not to draw vast conclusions from half-vast data.' His scientific restraint was admirable — but his famous exclamation spoke louder than his caution."
- Image Prompt: "Elderly Jerry Ehman in later years sitting in an office surrounded by astronomy books and memorabilia, the original Wow! printout framed on the wall behind him, wise and cautious expression, scientific integrity personified, cinematic photorealistic, 4K"
- Subtitle: "EHMAN: 'I CHOOSE NOT TO DRAW VAST CONCLUSIONS FROM HALF-VAST DATA'"
- Ken Burns: Slow zoom in on the framed printout behind him

**Scene 15**
- Narration: "Big Ear continued operating until nineteen ninety-seven when the land was sold for a golf course development — one of the most controversial decisions in the history of American science. The telescope that detected humanity's most intriguing signal from space was demolished to make room for fairways. The Wow! Signal's discovery site is now the eighteenth hole of a suburban golf course."
- Image Prompt: "Split image — on the left, the Big Ear radio telescope in its prime; on the right, the same location now showing a manicured golf course fairway, the transformation from science to recreation, jarring contrast, cinematic photorealistic, 4K"
- Subtitle: "BIG EAR DEMOLISHED IN 1997 — NOW A GOLF COURSE"

### CLIMAX (Scenes 16-22)

**Scene 16**
- Narration: "What makes the Wow! Signal so extraordinary is not just what it was — but what it was not. It was not broadband noise. It was not a known satellite frequency. It was not equipment interference. It was not terrestrial in origin. It passed every test for being a genuine extraterrestrial signal of artificial origin. The only thing it failed to do was repeat itself."
- Image Prompt: "Checklist board showing criteria for extraterrestrial signal detection — narrowband (check), correct frequency (check), non-terrestrial (check), correct beam pattern (check), repeatable (X) — all green checks except the last red X, cinematic photorealistic, 4K"
- Subtitle: "PASSED EVERY TEST — EXCEPT ONE: IT NEVER REPEATED"
- Ken Burns: Slow zoom in on the final red X

**Scene 17**
- Narration: "But should we expect an alien signal to repeat? If a civilization is broadcasting in all directions — a beacon — we would expect continuous or periodic signals. But what if it was not a beacon? What if it was a targeted transmission — a message sent to a specific destination that happened to cross our path for seventy-two seconds? We might have intercepted someone else's mail."
- Image Prompt: "Visualization of a narrow beam transmission traveling through space, Earth briefly passing through the beam as the planet orbits, the beam continuing past toward its actual destination, the chance interception illustrated, cinematic photorealistic, 4K"
- Subtitle: "WHAT IF WE INTERCEPTED SOMEONE ELSE'S TRANSMISSION?"
- Ken Burns: Slow pan following the beam past Earth

**Scene 18**
- Narration: "Another possibility: the signal was a one-time event from the source. A civilization's single attempt at contact — sent once and never repeated. Perhaps the civilization no longer exists. Perhaps the signal traveled for hundreds or thousands of years before reaching us. We heard an echo of something that ended long ago. A ghost signal from a dead civilization."
- Image Prompt: "Ruined alien architecture on a dead world — crumbling structures under a dim red sun, the remnants of a civilization that once existed but is now gone, the signal traveling outward from this dead world still, cinematic photorealistic, 4K"
- Subtitle: "A GHOST SIGNAL FROM A DEAD CIVILIZATION?"

While theoretically possible, no star has ever been observed producing a signal matching the Wow! Signal's characteristics."

- Subtitle: "STELLAR MASER THEORY — POSSIBLE BUT NEVER OBSERVED"

**Scene 20**
- Narration: "The SETI Institute has continued searching for similar signals with increasingly powerful equipment. The Allen Telescope Array, with forty-two dishes, monitors the sky continuously. No signal comparable to the Wow! Signal has been detected in nearly fifty years of searching. Either the signal was a one-time natural anomaly — or whatever produced it does not broadcast continuously."
- Image Prompt: "Array of modern radio telescope dishes in a desert setting, all pointed at the sky, the Allen Telescope Array, sunset light reflecting off the dishes, advanced technology dedicated to listening, cinematic photorealistic, 4K"
- Subtitle: "50 YEARS OF SEARCHING — NOTHING COMPARABLE DETECTED"

Signal originated. The star is similar in size, temperature, and age to our Sun. It is the only sun-like star in the signal's error box. If the signal was artificial — this star is the most likely origin."

- Narration: "The distance to this candidate star is approximately eighteen hundred light-years. If the signal was sent deliberately from a planet orbiting that star, it would have been transmitted approximately eighteen hundred years before we received it — around two hundred AD on Earth, during the Roman Empire. A message sent when Rome still stood — arriving as we develop the technology to hear it."
