### HOOK (Scenes 1-3)

**Scene 1**
- Narration: "On the morning of June seventh, nineteen ninety-two, three women disappeared from a house in Springfield, Missouri. A mother and her daughter's friend had been waiting up for the two girls to return from a graduation party. All three were gone by morning. The front door was locked. Their cars were in the driveway. Their purses, shoes, and cigarettes were inside. The only sign of disturbance was a broken porch light — the glass carefully swept into a neat pile. Someone had cleaned up after themselves."

No bodies. Nothing. For over thirty years — absolutely nothing."

The Springfield Three case suggests something terrifying: that whoever took them was someone they trusted enough to open the door for. Or someone skilled enough that trust was irrelevant."

**Scene 9**

In nineteen ninety-three, the state legislature passed a bill lowering the waiting period for missing person reports — eliminating the twenty-four-hour rule that had cost the investigation critical time. The bill was named for the three women. Their disappearance was not entirely without consequence — it just wasn't the consequence their families wanted."
