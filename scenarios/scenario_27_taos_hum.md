### HOOK (Scenes 1-3)

**Scene 1**
- Narration: "In the small town of Taos, New Mexico, approximately two percent of residents hear a persistent low-frequency humming sound that has no identifiable source. It has been reported since the early nineteen nineties. It is louder indoors than outdoors. It is worse at night. It disrupts sleep, causes headaches, and drives some people to the edge of madness. Scientific instruments detect nothing. Acoustic surveys find no source. Yet the sound persists — heard only by some, invisible to all equipment, and completely unexplained."
- Image Prompt: "Desert town of Taos, New Mexico at dusk, adobe buildings against a vast sky, the Sangre de Cristo Mountains in the background, a perfectly quiet-looking landscape that reportedly contains an invisible sound, cinematic photorealistic, 4K"
- Subtitle: "TAOS, NEW MEXICO — 2% OF RESIDENTS HEAR A SOUND — NO SOURCE FOUND"
- Ken Burns: Slow zoom toward the quiet town

**Scene 2**
- Narration: "The Taos Hum is not unique to Taos. Similar hums have been reported in Bristol, England. In Windsor, Ontario. In Auckland, New Zealand. In Largs, Scotland. Across dozens of locations worldwide, a small percentage of the population reports hearing a persistent low-frequency drone that no one else can hear and no instrument can detect. It is either a mass delusion affecting thousands independently — or a real phenomenon that exists below the threshold of our technology."
- Image Prompt: "World map showing locations of reported hum phenomena — Taos, Bristol, Windsor, Auckland, Largs — each marked with a sound wave symbol, the global distribution of an unexplained auditory phenomenon, cinematic photorealistic, 4K"
- Subtitle: "TAOS — BRISTOL — WINDSOR — AUCKLAND — A GLOBAL PHENOMENON"
- Ken Burns: Slow zoom out showing the global distribution

**Scene 3**
- Narration: "In nineteen ninety-three, residents of Taos petitioned the United States Congress for an investigation. Congress directed researchers from several institutions — including Sandia National Laboratories and the University of New Mexico — to study the phenomenon. After extensive testing, the researchers concluded: 'The hum is real to those who hear it. We cannot identify its source. We cannot detect it with instruments. We cannot explain it.' The investigation ended. The hum did not."
- Image Prompt: "Congressional hearing room with scientists presenting findings to committee members, acoustic equipment displayed as evidence, the institutional acknowledgment of something they cannot explain, formal government setting, cinematic photorealistic, 4K"
- Subtitle: "1993 — CONGRESS INVESTIGATED — 'THE HUM IS REAL — WE CANNOT EXPLAIN IT'"

### SETUP (Scenes 4-8)

**Scene 4**
- Narration: "Taos is located in northern New Mexico at an elevation of seven thousand feet, surrounded by the Sangre de Cristo Mountains. It is home to approximately six thousand people and has been an artistic community for over a century. The environment is quiet — minimal industrial activity, low traffic, vast open spaces. It is one of the quietest places in the continental United States. Which makes the reported hum all the more disturbing — there is nothing here that should produce it."
- Image Prompt: "Panoramic view of Taos Valley — adobe architecture, art galleries, vast desert landscape, mountains rising behind, the serenity of a quiet artistic community, no factories, no highways, no obvious sound sources, cinematic photorealistic, 4K"
- Subtitle: "ONE OF THE QUIETEST PLACES IN AMERICA — NOTHING SHOULD PRODUCE A HUM"
- Ken Burns: Slow pan across the quiet valley

**Scene 5**
- Narration: "Those who hear the Taos Hum describe it consistently: a low-frequency drone resembling a diesel engine idling in the distance. It pulsates slightly. It is between thirty and eighty hertz — near the lower limit of human hearing. It is louder at night. It is louder indoors. It is not masked by background noise — sometimes it seems louder when the environment is quieter. Earplugs do not help. Nothing helps."
- Image Prompt: "Person lying awake in bed at night, hands over ears, expression of distress, the bedroom silent to all observation but clearly unbearable to the person, the isolation of hearing something no one else can, nighttime desert visible through window, cinematic photorealistic, 4K"

- Ken Burns: Slow zoom in on the person's distressed face

**Scene 6**
- Narration: "The phenomenon affects only about two percent of the population in any given location. This selectivity is one of its most puzzling features. If the hum were an external sound, everyone with normal hearing should detect it. The fact that only some people hear it suggests either: a real sound at a frequency most people cannot perceive, an internal physiological phenomenon in sensitive individuals, or a neurological condition that creates the perception of sound without external stimulus."
- Image Prompt: "Group of people standing together, most looking calm, but one or two with visible discomfort pressing hands to their temples, the invisible division between hearers and non-hearers, the social isolation of perceiving something others don't, cinematic photorealistic, 4K"
- Subtitle: "ONLY 2% HEAR IT — IF EXTERNAL, WHY NOT EVERYONE? — THE CENTRAL PUZZLE"
- Ken Burns: Slow zoom in on the affected individuals

**Scene 7**
- Narration: "The Sandia National Laboratories investigation in nineteen ninety-three used the most sensitive acoustic equipment available — including military-grade low-frequency microphones and vibration sensors. They detected no anomalous low-frequency sound in the Taos environment. The measurements showed normal background noise for a rural community. Whatever the 'hearers' were perceiving — it was not registering on instruments designed to detect exactly this type of sound."
- Image Prompt: "Sensitive acoustic monitoring equipment deployed in the Taos landscape — specialized microphones, seismometers, spectrum analyzers — all showing flat readings in the low-frequency range, the instruments finding nothing, desert setting, cinematic photorealistic, 4K"
- Subtitle: "MILITARY-GRADE EQUIPMENT — FLAT READINGS — NOTHING DETECTED"

**Scene 8**
- Narration: "Researchers tested the hearers themselves. They confirmed that the subjects were not fabricating — physiological stress markers were consistent with prolonged noise exposure. Some hearers could identify when the hum was 'louder' or 'softer' consistently over time. A few could detect the direction it came from. The experience was genuine. The hearers were real people with a real perception. The question was: perception of what?"
- Image Prompt: "Medical testing facility with a subject wearing monitoring equipment, audiologist conducting tests, brain activity displayed on a screen, the genuine physiological response to something undetectable, clinical and scientific, cinematic photorealistic, 4K"
- Subtitle: "THE HEARERS ARE GENUINE — PHYSIOLOGICAL STRESS CONFIRMED — PERCEPTION OF WHAT?"

### RISING ACTION (Scenes 9-15)

**Scene 9**
- Narration: "One hypothesis involves otoacoustic emissions — sounds generated by the inner ear itself. The human cochlea is not merely a receiver — it actively generates faint sounds as part of its amplification process. In some individuals, these internally generated sounds may become perceptible — particularly in quiet environments. The 'hum' might be the sound of their own ears working — amplified by silence."
- Image Prompt: "Detailed anatomical illustration of the human inner ear — cochlea, hair cells, the mechanism of otoacoustic emission, the ear as both receiver and generator of sound, medical precision, cinematic photorealistic, 4K"
- Subtitle: "THE EAR GENERATES SOUNDS — SOME PEOPLE MAY HEAR THEIR OWN EARS"
- Ken Burns: Slow zoom into the cochlear structure

**Scene 10**
- Narration: "Another theory proposes very low frequency electromagnetic radiation — power grid harmonics, military communications, or geological piezoelectric effects — that some individuals can perceive as sound through a mechanism not yet understood. The human body contains iron and other conductors that might theoretically resonate with electromagnetic fields — converting EM energy into perceived vibration."
- Image Prompt: "Power grid infrastructure and underground geological layers both generating invisible electromagnetic fields, the human body positioned between them receiving signals, the theoretical mechanism of EM perception, scientific visualization, cinematic photorealistic, 4K"
- Subtitle: "ELECTROMAGNETIC FIELDS? — POWER GRIDS? — GEOLOGY? — SOME BODIES RESONATE?"
- Ken Burns: Slow pan through the electromagnetic environment

**Scene 11**
- Narration: "In Bristol, England — where the hum has been reported since the nineteen seventies — researcher Geoff Leventhall concluded that at least some hearers are perceiving real low-frequency sound from distant industrial sources amplified by atmospheric conditions. Low-frequency sound can travel hundreds of miles and be channeled by temperature inversions. A factory fifty miles away might be inaudible to most but perceptible to those with acute low-frequency sensitivity."
- Image Prompt: "Cross-section of atmosphere showing a temperature inversion layer trapping and channeling low-frequency sound waves from a distant factory across many miles to a residential area, the physics of long-distance sound propagation, cinematic photorealistic, 4K"
- Subtitle: "BRISTOL: DISTANT INDUSTRY — ATMOSPHERIC CHANNELING — 50+ MILES"
- Ken Burns: Slow pan following the sound propagation path

**Scene 12**
- Narration: "The geological theory is particularly relevant to Taos. The area sits on a volcanic plateau above a deep aquifer. Underground water movement through porous volcanic rock can generate infrasound — sound below twenty hertz — that is felt rather than heard. The steady flow of water through underground channels could create a persistent drone that sensitive individuals perceive as a hum. The earth itself might be humming."
- Image Prompt: "Geological cross-section of the Taos area showing volcanic rock layers above a deep aquifer, water flowing through underground channels, the generation of infrasound from geological processes, the earth as sound source, cinematic photorealistic, 4K"
- Subtitle: "UNDERGROUND WATER THROUGH VOLCANIC ROCK — THE EARTH ITSELF HUMMING"
- Ken Burns: Slow zoom into the underground water flow

**Scene 13**
- Narration: "Tinnitus — ringing in the ears — is a well-known condition affecting approximately fifteen percent of the population. But tinnitus typically presents as a high-pitched tone, not a low-frequency hum. Some audiologists have proposed that the Taos Hum represents a low-frequency variant of tinnitus — the brain generating the perception of bass-range sound without external input. If so, it is a neurological condition, not an environmental one."
- Image Prompt: "Comparison between standard tinnitus (high-frequency waveform) and the reported hum (low-frequency waveform), the audiological comparison, medical charts and hearing test results, clinical presentation, cinematic photorealistic, 4K"
- Subtitle: "LOW-FREQUENCY TINNITUS? — THE BRAIN CREATING SOUND WITHOUT INPUT?"
- Ken Burns: Slow zoom comparing the waveforms

**Scene 14**
- Narration: "What argues against the purely neurological explanation is geography. If the hum were internal, it should follow the individual — not the location. But many Taos hearers report that the hum stops when they leave the area and returns when they come back. This geographic specificity suggests an external source — something present in Taos but not in other locations. An internal condition would not respect state borders."
- Image Prompt: "Map showing a person's travel route — the hum present in Taos, absent in Albuquerque, absent in Santa Fe, present again upon return — the geographic specificity illustrated, the pattern that suggests external source, cinematic photorealistic, 4K"
- Subtitle: "THE HUM STOPS WHEN THEY LEAVE — RETURNS WHEN THEY COME BACK — EXTERNAL?"

**Scene 15**
- Narration: "In two thousand twelve, a French research team proposed that some hum reports worldwide correlate with ocean microseism — persistent low-frequency vibration generated by ocean waves interacting with the continental shelf. This vibration travels through the Earth's crust and produces infrasound at specific inland locations. Taos's elevation and geological structure might amplify this signal. The ocean — three hundred miles away — might be what they hear."
- Image Prompt: "Ocean waves generating vibration that travels through the continental crust inland, the seismic energy moving through rock layers and emerging at specific geographic locations, the ocean's influence reaching the desert, cinematic photorealistic, 4K"
