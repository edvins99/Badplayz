### HOOK (Scenes 1-3)

- Narration: "On March thirty-first, nineteen twenty-two, six people were murdered on an isolated farm in Bavaria, Germany. A family of four, their maid, and a two-year-old child — all bludgeoned to death with a mattock. But the most disturbing detail was not the murders themselves. It was the evidence that the killer had remained on the farm for days after the killings — feeding the livestock, eating the food, and sleeping in the house among the dead. And the evidence that someone had been hiding in the attic before the murders began."

### SETUP (Scenes 4-8)

**Scene 4**
- Narration: "Hinterkaifeck was a small, isolated farmstead located between the towns of Ingolstadt and Schrobenhausen in Bavaria — about seventy kilometers north of Munich. The nearest neighbor was over half a kilometer away. The farm was surrounded by dense forest on three sides. It was the kind of place where someone could scream — and no one would hear."

### RISING ACTION (Scenes 9-15)

**Scene 9**
- Narration: "The murders were not discovered until April fourth — four days later. When Gruber failed to appear at a meeting, neighbors went to check on the family. They found the farm eerily normal at first glance: livestock had been fed, the dog was alive, smoke stains suggested the fire had been lit recently. But the house was silent. And the barn door was slightly open."

**Scene 10**
- Narration: "In the barn, stacked neatly under hay, they found four bodies: Andreas, Cäzilia senior, Viktoria, and young Cäzilia. All had been killed with a mattock — a pickaxe-like farming tool. The seven-year-old girl showed signs of having survived for several hours after the attack — pulling her own hair out in clumps as she lay dying beside her dead family. The hay had been placed over them deliberately — they had been covered."

Someone had lived among six corpses — tending the animals, cooking meals, sleeping under the same roof as the dead — as if nothing had happened."

### CLIMAX (Scenes 16-22)

**Scene 16**
- Narration: "The investigation was massive by nineteen twenties standards. Over one hundred people were questioned. Suspicion fell on many: a former farmhand with a grudge. Andreas's son from a previous relationship. Local men who had been rejected by Viktoria. A traveling craftsman who had been seen in the area. Each had motive. None could be definitively connected to the evidence."

**Scene 17**
- Narration: "The strongest suspect was Karl Gabriel — Viktoria's husband — who had been officially declared dead in World War One. His body was never recovered from the Western Front. In the chaotic aftermath of the war, many soldiers declared dead were later found alive. If Karl returned to find his wife had borne his father-in-law's child — the rage could have been sufficient. But proof was impossible to obtain."

- Narration: "Another theory pointed to Lorenz Schlittenbauer — a neighbor who had been intimate with Viktoria and was likely the biological father of Josef. He was one of the first to 'discover' the bodies."

Each investigation reached the same conclusion: insufficient evidence to prosecute any individual. The killer — whoever they were — executed the crime and their escape with equal perfection."

- Narration: "The two thousand seven student investigation concluded — with modern profiling techniques — that Schlittenbauer was the most likely perpetrator. His motive was strongest: financial gain from the farm, anger over paternity deception, and intimate knowledge of the family's routines. His behavior at the crime scene was consistent with guilty knowledge. But 'most likely' is not proof. And Schlittenbauer died in nineteen forty-one — taking whatever he knew to his own grave."

- Narration: "The heads of the six victims were removed during the original autopsy and sent to Munich for examination by clairvoyants — a practice that was not unusual in nineteen twenties Germany. The skulls were never returned to the bodies. They were stored in various locations until they were reportedly destroyed during the bombing of Munich in World War Two. Even in death, the victims of Hinterkaifeck were denied peace."

### FALLING ACTION (Scenes 23-29)

**Scene 23**
- Narration: "The farm was demolished in nineteen twenty-three — just one year after the murders. The land was sold. No trace of the buildings remains today. The site is now an empty field beside a memorial stone. Without the physical crime scene, any future re-investigation is limited to documents, photographs, and testimony. The primary evidence was destroyed before modern forensics existed."

**Scene 24**
- Narration: "The mattock — the murder weapon — was recovered and photographed. It was a common agricultural tool available on any Bavarian farm. No fingerprints were preserved — forensic techniques of the era did not prioritize them. The weapon told investigators nothing except that the killer used what was available rather than bringing their own. This supported the theory that the killer was familiar with the farm."

She was conscious and in agony beside the bodies of her family. And no one came. Either the killer was aware she was alive and left her to die — or they had already departed before she woke. ### CONCLUSION (Scenes 30-32)

**Scene 30**
- Narration: "Six people died at Hinterkaifeck on a March night in nineteen twenty-two. An elderly couple. Their daughter. A child of seven. A baby of two. A maid on her first day. Their killer was never caught. Never identified. Never punished. They walked out of that farm — past the footprints in the snow — and disappeared into a world that has spent a century trying to find them."
